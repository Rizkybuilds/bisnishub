# 03. CANDIDATE A — THE ARCHITECTURE OF HYPOCRISY

* **Pillar:** Moral Contradictions
* **Score:** **88/100**
* **Decision:** **ADVANCE TO FULL RESEARCH**

## A. Strategic Opportunity
The broad subject is moral inconsistency, self-judgment, perceived hypocrisy, and the different standards people may apply to themselves and others. It directly expresses the Human Condition worldview: *We are willing to examine contradictions in other people while remaining imperfect observers of our own behavior.*

## B. Research Opportunity (Desk Research Findings)

### 1. Research Direction 1 — Recognizing Inconsistency in Ourselves and Others
* **Voorhoeve (2025):** Two preregistered online experiments using samples broadly representative of the UK population ($N = 814$ and $N = 1,623$).
* **Core Finding:** Participants were significantly more likely to recognize inconsistencies in other people's moral judgments than in their own.
* **Mechanism Uncertainty:** The research did not establish a complete psychological explanation for this difference. Motivated reasoning, selective cognitive effort, and limited insight received only partial or no support.
* **Strategic Value:** The finding itself is empirically solid while its psychological mechanism remains open—an ideal territory for investigative editorial.

### 2. Research Direction 2 — How People Decide Whether Something Is Hypocrisy
* **Silver and Berman (2024):** Three preregistered experiments involving $2,599$ participants.
* **Core Finding:** Examined *perceived comparability*: whether two situations are considered sufficiently similar for a charge of hypocrisy to be valid. People's judgments of comparability shift systematically with social motives to criticize enemies or defend allies.
* **Strategic Value:** Provides a distinct and nuanced Human Condition angle beyond simple moralizing.

### 3. Research Direction 3 — The Limits of Generalizing Moral Hypocrisy
* **Robertson, Akles, and Van Bavel (2024):** Preregistered replication and extension of earlier research into intergroup moral hypocrisy.
* **Core Finding:** Results did not replicate original findings in a uniform way; produced mixed evidence depending on group context.
* **Strategic Value:** Critical counter-evidence preventing simplistic claims that people always excuse their in-group and judge outsiders more harshly.

## C. Existing Knowledge Landscape
The subject has established academic foundations across:
- Moral psychology, moral inconsistency, self-judgment, moral identity, motivated reasoning, social identity, perceived comparability, and moral evaluation.
- Review by **Ellemers et al.:** Examined $1,278$ empirical studies of morality published between 1940 and 2017.

## D. Competitive Landscape
* **Current Presence:** Association for Psychological Science (APS 2024) feature *"Making Sense of Moral Hypocrisy"*; Yale's *Philosophy and the Science of Human Nature* course material on Plato's Ring of Gyges.
* **Competitive Audit:** Preliminary public search shows no single high-reach creator dominating this exact cognitive mechanism question.

## E. Proposed Differentiation
Do not make a general accusation that human beings are hypocrites. Investigate the mechanisms by which people identify, excuse, or interpret inconsistency.

* **Recommended Working Title:** **THE HYPOCRISY MIRROR**
* **Subtitle:** *Why We See Inconsistency in Others Before Ourselves.*
* **Central Editorial Question:** *Why are people sometimes more likely to recognize inconsistencies in other people's moral judgments than their own, and how do they decide when two situations are comparable enough to justify calling someone a hypocrite?*
* **Working Thesis:** *Judgments of hypocrisy are influenced not only by differences between stated principles and behavior, but also by how observers interpret their own judgments, compare situations, and evaluate the people involved. Not every apparent inconsistency constitutes hypocrisy.*
* **Important Distinctions (Mandatory Conceptual Boundaries):**
  1. Moral hypocrisy.
  2. Moral inconsistency.
  3. Different treatment under genuinely different circumstances.
  4. Deliberate deception.
  5. Self-justification.
  6. Socially motivated accusations.

## F. Strategic Scoring
* Brand relevance: **5** (Directly expresses self-awareness and human contradiction)
* Audience relevance: **3** (Broadly relatable, direct demand unvalidated)
* Differentiation: **4** (Strong opportunity through self-vs-other judgment and comparability)
* Intellectual depth: **5** (Multiple established empirical and philosophical streams)
* Storytelling potential: **5** (Relatable conflicts and strong conceptual metaphors)
* Long-term potential: **5** (Can expand into several interconnected investigations and apparel capsule `HC-002`)
* **Total Score:** **88/100**

## G. Risks
- Moralistic storytelling or preaching.
- Treating every inconsistency as hypocrisy.
- Inferring motives without empirical evidence.
- Oversimplifying political or group identity effects.
- Using real-world examples that become unsupported personal allegations.

# 04. CANDIDATE B — THE POWER OF THE CROWD

* **Pillar:** The Social Animal
* **Score:** **85/100**
* **Decision:** **ADVANCE WITH REFRAMING**

## A. Strategic Opportunity
Explores how perceived majority opinions and social groups influence individual judgment and expression.

## B. Research Opportunity
1. **Wijenayake and Goncalves (2025):** 36-year literature review on online social conformity (1988–2023), confirming it as an established field.
2. **Goglia, Vega, and Gandelli (2026):** Majority influence in anonymous, spontaneous online conversations; identified *anti-conformity behavior* where participants retained the majority's polarity while diverging from specific positions.
3. **Social and Algorithmic Norms:** Experiments on how algorithmic recommendations shape perceived social consensus.

## C. Proposed Differentiation & Repositioning
* Move beyond: *"Why do people follow the crowd?"*
* Investigate: **THE INVISIBLE MAJORITY: How perceived consensus shapes what we think other people believe.**
* Working thesis: Group influence depends not only on actual belief distribution, but on visibility, expression, and algorithmic sorting.

## D. Strategic Scoring
* Brand relevance: **5** | Audience relevance: **3** | Differentiation: **4** | Intellectual depth: **5** | Storytelling potential: **4** | Long-term potential: **5**
* **Total Score:** **85/100**

## E. Recommendation
