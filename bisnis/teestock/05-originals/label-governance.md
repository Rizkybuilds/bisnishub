# 1. Purpose

Label Governance menjawab:

> **Bagaimana TeeStock membangun banyak consumer brands tanpa menciptakan kekacauan brand, operasi, teknologi, dan organisasi?**

Canonical principle:

> **Autonomy is earned. Infrastructure is shared by default.**

---

# 2. Canonical Definition

> **Independent Label adalah consumer brand di dalam TeeStock Originals yang telah membuktikan belief, audience, identity, product universe, commercial demand, dan long-term strategic potential yang cukup untuk memiliki brand identity serta operating autonomy yang lebih besar daripada sebuah Collection.**

---

# 3. Independent Label ≠ Separate Company

Critical distinction:

```text id="lbl001"
LABEL
consumer brand identity

BUSINESS UNIT
organizational / economic unit

LEGAL ENTITY
legal organization
```

Sebuah Label tidak membutuhkan perusahaan terpisah hanya untuk menjadi brand yang valid.

---

# 4. Independent Label ≠ Collection

```text id="lbl002"
COLLECTION
tests a creative idea.

LABEL
owns a repeatable consumer world.
```

Label dapat menghasilkan banyak Collections.

Collection tidak otomatis memiliki Label-level autonomy.

---

# 5. Independent Label ≠ TeeStock Master Brand

Canonical relationship:

```text id="lbl003"
TEEStock
master ecosystem / trust / infrastructure

LABEL
consumer identity / culture / community
```

---

# 6. Governance Objective

Label Governance harus menjaga keseimbangan antara:

```text id="lbl004"
CREATIVE AUTONOMY
+
COMMERCIAL DISCIPLINE
+
SHARED INFRASTRUCTURE
+
PORTFOLIO COHERENCE
```

---

# 7. Why Governance Is Necessary

Tanpa governance, portfolio berisiko menghasilkan:

- terlalu banyak brand,
- duplicate domains,
- duplicate social accounts,
- fragmented customer data,
- duplicated inventory,
- unclear IP ownership,
- confused marketing,
- founder approval bottleneck,
- zombie brands.

---

# 8. Canonical Label Promotion Standard

Collection hanya boleh menjadi Label jika ada evidence terhadap:

```text id="lbl005"
BELIEF
AUDIENCE
IDENTITY
PRODUCT DEPTH
REPEAT DEMAND
COMMUNITY POTENTIAL
ECONOMICS
OPERABILITY
```

---

# 9. Minimum Label Characteristics

A Label should have:

```text id="lbl006"
Distinct Belief
Defined Audience
Own Voice
Own Visual Language
Own Product Principles
Multiple Collection Potential
Long-Term Narrative
Portfolio Role
```

---

# 10. Belief

Label harus bisa menjawab:

> **What does this brand believe?**

Bukan hanya:

> “Apa aesthetic-nya?”

---

# 11. Audience

Label harus tahu:

> **Who sees themselves in this brand?**

Audience bukan hanya demographic segment.

---

# 12. Worldview

Worldview adalah cara Label melihat:

- progress,
- identity,
- work,
- culture,
- lifestyle,
- belonging,

tergantung territory-nya.

---

# 13. Visual Identity

Label memerlukan enough distinctiveness untuk dikenali tanpa selalu bergantung pada TeeStock visual identity.

---

# 14. Voice

Label dapat memiliki voice sendiri.

Namun:

```text id="lbl007"
LABEL VOICE
must not contradict
TEEStock legal / customer truth standards
```

---

# 15. Product Universe

Label harus mempunyai realistic path menuju:

```text id="lbl008"
MULTIPLE PRODUCTS
+
MULTIPLE COLLECTIONS
```

Tidak harus semuanya diproduksi sekarang.

---

# 16. Portfolio Role

Setiap Label harus mempunyai alasan keberadaan yang berbeda dalam TeeStock portfolio.

Canonical question:

> **Why does this Label exist if another TeeStock Label already exists?**

---

# 17. Label Autonomy Model

Canonical autonomy ladder:

```text id="lbl009"
L0 — COLLECTION
L1 — ENDORSED LABEL
L2 — SEMI-INDEPENDENT LABEL
L3 — INDEPENDENT FRONTEND LABEL
L4 — INDEPENDENT BUSINESS UNIT
```

---

# 18. L0 — Collection

Not technically Independent Label yet.

Characteristics:

```text id="lbl010"
TeeStock Originals identity dominant
Shared storefront
Shared social
Shared infrastructure
No dedicated team
No separate domain
```

Purpose:

> validation.

---

# 19. L1 — Endorsed Label

First formal Label level.

Customer-facing form:

```text id="lbl011"
[LABEL NAME]
A TEEStock ORIGINAL
```

---

# 20. L1 Characteristics

```text id="lbl012"
Distinct Brand Name
Distinct Visual Identity
Distinct Voice
Dedicated Label Page
Own Collections
Shared TeeStock Storefront
Shared Checkout
Shared Customer Ops
Shared Fulfillment
Shared MGBOS
```

---

# 21. L1 Strategic Purpose

Borrow TeeStock:

- trust,
- operations,
- distribution,

while Label builds:

- awareness,
- identity,
- audience.

---

# 22. L2 — Semi-Independent Label

A proven Label may gain greater consumer-facing autonomy.

Possible:

```text id="lbl013"
Dedicated Social Account
Dedicated Content Calendar
Label-Specific Campaigns
Distinct CRM Segmentation
Expanded Product Roadmap
```

Backend remains shared.

---

# 23. L2 Requirements

Generally requires:

```text id="lbl014"
REPEATED RELEASES
+
REPEATED DEMAND
+
CONTENT DEPTH
+
BRAND RECALL
+
OPERATIONAL STABILITY
```

---

# 24. L3 — Independent Frontend Label

A mature Label may operate its own customer-facing digital experience.

Possible:

```text id="lbl015"
Own Domain
Own Website / Frontend
Own Merchandising Experience
Own Customer-Facing Brand Journey
```

---

# 25. L3 Backend

Default remains:

```text id="lbl016"
MGBOS
Catalog
Inventory
Payment
Customer Identity
Fulfillment
Analytics
```

shared unless compelling reason exists otherwise.

---

# 26. L3 Principle

Canonical:

> **Independent frontend. Shared operating backbone.**

---

# 27. L4 — Independent Business Unit

Highest autonomy.

Potential:

```text id="lbl017"
Dedicated Leadership
Dedicated P&L
Dedicated Team
Independent Budget
Independent Roadmap
Possible Separate Legal Structure
```

This is exceptional.

---

# 28. L4 Is Not a Goal for Every Label

Some Labels may remain highly profitable at:

```text id="lbl018"
L1
or
L2
```

indefinitely.

Autonomy is not prestige.

It is operating design.

---

# 29. Autonomy Promotion Principle

Canonical:

```text id="lbl019"
MORE AUTONOMY
ONLY WHEN
AUTONOMY CREATES MORE VALUE
THAN SHARED INFRASTRUCTURE
```

---

# 30. Autonomy Dimensions

A Label can gain autonomy independently across:

```text id="lbl020"
BRAND
CONTENT
CHANNEL
PRODUCT
BUDGET
TEAM
TECH
OPERATIONS
LEGAL
```

Autonomy need not increase equally across all dimensions.

---

# 31. Brand Autonomy

Controls:

- voice,
- visual identity,
- campaigns,
- brand storytelling.

Usually granted earlier.

---

# 32. Product Autonomy

Controls:

- product roadmap,
- collection direction,
- proprietary garment development.

Earned after demand and product maturity.

---

# 33. Content Autonomy

Controls:

- social,
- editorial,
- creator relationships,
- community programming.

Requires publishing capacity.

---

# 34. Commercial Autonomy

Controls:

- campaign pricing,
- promotions,
- marketing budget.

Requires guardrails and economics.

---

# 35. Operational Autonomy

Controls:

- sourcing,
- production,
- inventory,
- fulfillment.

Should be granted much later.

Shared operations usually create more leverage.

---

# 36. Technology Autonomy

Own frontend may come before own backend.

Do not duplicate systems unnecessarily.

---

# 37. Legal Autonomy

Separate entity should come only from:

- risk,
- investment,
- partnership,
- taxation,
- governance,

requirements.

Not aesthetic preference.

---

# 38. Endorsement Architecture

Default new Label relationship:

```text id="lbl021"
[LABEL]
A TEEStock ORIGINAL
```

---

# 39. Endorsement Purpose

Communicates:

```text id="lbl022"
ORIGIN
+
TEEStock TRUST
+
PORTFOLIO RELATIONSHIP
```

---

# 40. Endorsement Visibility

Visibility can decrease as Label gains independent recognition.

Possible ladder:

```text id="lbl023"
PROMINENT
↓
SECONDARY
↓
SUBTLE
↓
BACKEND / ABOUT ONLY
```

---

# 41. Endorsement Removal

Removal should happen only when independent Label trust is sufficient.

Not simply because Label wants to “look more premium.”

---

# 42. Master Brand Protection

A Label using TeeStock endorsement must meet:

```text id="lbl024"
QUALITY
LEGAL
CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE
ETHICAL
OPERATIONAL
```

minimum standards.

---

# 43. Label Naming

A Label name should be:

```text id="lbl025"
DISTINCT
EXTENDABLE
MEMORABLE
DEFENSIBLE
```

---

# 44. Working Name vs Activated Label Name

Canonical:

```text id="lbl026"
CONCEPT NAME
≠
APPROVED LABEL NAME
```

---

# 45. Trademark Gate

Before Label activation:

perform appropriate current trademark/name availability review.

---

# 46. Trademark Ownership

Where legally practical:

core Label trademarks should be controlled by the appropriate TeeStock/MultiGraph entity defined by legal architecture.

Do not leave strategic brand ownership personally fragmented.

---

# 47. Domain Governance

Domain should be registered under controlled organizational ownership.

Avoid critical Label domains being owned only by:

- freelancer,
- employee,
- outside agency.

---

# 48. Social Account Governance

Credentials/ownership must remain organizationally controlled.

A departing team member must not take Label channels with them.

---

# 49. Social Account Creation Gate

Dedicated Label account only if:

```text id="lbl027"
CONTENT DEPTH
+
PUBLISHING CAPACITY
+
AUDIENCE REASON
+
LONG-TERM LABEL COMMITMENT
```

---

# 50. No Dead Accounts

Canonical:

> **No social account is better than an abandoned social account.**

---

# 51. Domain Creation Gate

Own domain only if:

```text id="lbl028"
DIRECT DEMAND
+
BRAND DEPTH
+
LONG-TERM ROADMAP
+
COMMERCIAL VALUE
```

---

# 52. Website vs Domain

A Label can have a dedicated website experience under TeeStock domain without owning separate domain.

Example:

```text id="lbl029"
teestock.id/originals/[label]
```

This is preferred earlier.

---

# 53. Customer Identity

All Labels should initially share:

```text id="lbl030"
ONE CANONICAL CUSTOMER IDENTITY
```

within TeeStock/MGBOS.

---

# 54. Label Customer Affinity

System can separately track:

```text id="lbl031"
Customer
→ Label A affinity
→ Label B affinity
```

without duplicating customer record.

---

# 55. Customer Data Governance

Label teams receive only data appropriate to their role and privacy policy.

Brand autonomy does not mean unrestricted access to all ecosystem customer data.

---

# 56. Label P&L

Every activated Label should progressively gain visible economics.

At minimum:

```text id="lbl032"
NET SALES
COGS
PRODUCTION
FULFILLMENT
MARKETING
CREATIVE
CONTRIBUTION
INVENTORY
```

---

# 57. Shared Cost Allocation

Shared TeeStock infrastructure may allocate:

- fulfillment,
- Studio,
- technology,
- customer ops,
- administration,

for management reporting.

---

# 58. Management P&L ≠ Legal Accounting Entity

Label-level P&L can exist without separate company.

---

# 59. Label Budget

Budget should cover:

```text id="lbl033"
PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT
SAMPLES
CONTENT
MARKETING
INVENTORY
CREATIVE
```

based on maturity.

---

# 60. Budget Authority

Higher autonomy can grant Label Steward defined budget authority.

Spending above thresholds escalates.

---

# 61. Capital Allocation

TeeStock portfolio allocates capital based on:

```text id="lbl034"
EVIDENCE
+
OPPORTUNITY
+
RETURN POTENTIAL
+
PORTFOLIO STRATEGY
```

---

# 62. Label Steward

Each meaningful Label should eventually have:

```text id="lbl035"
LABEL STEWARD
```

---

# 63. Label Steward Responsibilities

Owns:

```text id="lbl036"
Belief
Identity
Product Direction
Collection Roadmap
Content Direction
Brand Consistency
Performance Review
```

---

# 64. Label Steward Does Not Own Everything

Shared teams may still own:

- production,
- finance,
- fulfillment,
- legal,
- technology.

---

# 65. Portfolio Owner

Portfolio Owner oversees:

```text id="lbl037"
PROMOTION
RESOURCE ALLOCATION
PAUSE
ARCHIVE
AUTONOMY
SPIN-OUT
```

across Labels.

---

# 66. Founder Role

Early founder may be Portfolio Owner and Label Steward.

Long-term these roles should be separable.

---

# 67. Approval Rights

Approval authority should be explicit.

Possible areas:

```text id="lbl038"
Creative
Product
Price
Campaign
Budget
Collaboration
Legal
Inventory
```

---

# 68. Creative Approval

L1 may require more TeeStock/portfolio oversight.

L2/L3 can give Label Steward more authority within Brand Bible.

---

# 69. Product Approval

Product approval requires both:

```text id="lbl039"
BRAND FIT
+
OPERATIONAL FEASIBILITY
```

Label cannot launch products that operations cannot reliably support.

---

# 70. Pricing Approval

Label may recommend price.

Final pricing must respect:

```text id="lbl040"
UNIT ECONOMICS
PRICE FLOOR
PORTFOLIO POLICY
```

---

# 71. Discount Approval

Discounts beyond approved range require escalation.

Brand team cannot solve every sales problem with promotions.

---

# 72. Inventory Approval

Large finished-goods investment should require:

```text id="lbl041"
DEMAND EVIDENCE
+
CAPITAL APPROVAL
```

---

# 73. Collaboration Approval

Collaboration should consider:

```text id="lbl042"
BRAND FIT
IP
ECONOMICS
REPUTATION
OPERATIONS
```

---

# 74. Legal Approval

Trademark, licensing, major contracts, or material IP issues require appropriate legal review.

---

# 75. Label Brand Bible

```text id="lbl043"
Symbols
```

# 76. Brand Bible Purpose

```text id="lbl044"
FOUNDER APPROVING EVERYTHING
```

# 77. Belief Governance

Label must not casually change core belief because a trend performs well.

Major belief change may represent:

- repositioning,
- or a new Concept.

---

# 78. Audience Governance

Audience understanding can evolve.

But Label should not chase completely unrelated segments each Collection.

---

# 79. Voice Governance

Voice can evolve without losing core personality.

---

# 80. Visual Governance

Collection may explore.

Label remains recognizable.

Canonical balance:

```text id="lbl045"
CONSISTENCY
+
EVOLUTION
```

---

# 81. Product Governance

Each Label should define:

```text id="lbl046"
WHAT WE MAKE
WHAT WE MIGHT MAKE
WHAT WE DO NOT MAKE
```

---

# 82. Product Opportunity Filter

Before entering new product category:

```text id="lbl047"
Does audience want it?

Does it express the Label?

Can TeeStock execute it?

Does economics work?
```

---

# 83. Product Line Expansion

Canonical progression:

```text id="lbl048"
PROVEN CATEGORY
↓
ADJACENT CATEGORY
↓
NEW PRODUCT SYSTEM
```

Avoid random category hopping.

---

# 84. Proprietary Garment Governance

Label-specific garment development requires:

```text id="lbl049"
+
IDENTITY VALUE

VOLUME

CAPITAL
+
SUPPLY CAPABILITY
```

---

# 85. Shared Garment Default

Before proprietary product becomes justified:

use TeeStock shared Garment Platforms.

---

# 86. Collection Governance

A Label can have multiple Collections.

```text id="lbl050"
TS-ORG-003 Collection Framework
```

---

# 87. Collection Calendar

Label Steward should maintain:

```text id="lbl051"
DEVELOPMENT
LAUNCH
EVERGREEN
CAMPAIGN
REVIEW
```

calendar.

---

# 88. Release Frequency

Do not force arbitrary frequency.

Release only when:

- product ready,
- story meaningful,
- demand/strategy supports it.

---

# 89. Content Governance

Label content should map to defined territories.

Example:

```text id="lbl052"
BELIEF
CULTURE
PRODUCT
PEOPLE
PROCESS
```

---

# 90. Content Independence

At L2+, Label may have own publishing rhythm.

But operational/product facts still derive from canonical TeeStock data.

---

# 91. Community Governance

until participation exists.

---

# 92. Community Assets

# 96. Support Tone

# 97. Fulfillment

# 98. Supply

```text id="lbl063"
AUDIENCE

PRODUCT

FINANCE
```

---


---

# 116. Audience Health


---

# 119. Operational Health

```text id="lbl068"

Is complexity manageable?
```

---

# 120. Financial Health

Ask:

```text id="lbl069"

```

---

# 121. Label Review Cadence

Potential:

```text id="lbl070"

portfolio / budget review

ANNUALLY
strategic autonomy review
```

Exact cadence may evolve.

---

# 122. Autonomy Review

Autonomy should not increase automatically.

Review whether:

```text id="lbl071"
MORE AUTONOMY
would improve
BRAND / ECONOMICS / EXPERIENCE
```

---

# 123. Autonomy Can Decrease

If separate complexity no longer creates value:

Label may return to more shared systems.

Autonomy is reversible.

---

# 124. Label Promotion

Potential:

```text id="lbl072"
L1 → L2
L2 → L3
L3 → L4
```

requires formal decision.

---

# 125. Label Demotion

Possible if:

- demand weakens,
- team disappears,
- content cannot be sustained,
- economics deteriorate.

Could reduce autonomy without immediately archiving Label.

---

# 126. Pause Decision

Pause when:

```text id="lbl073"
CORE IP STILL VALUABLE
+
CURRENT EXECUTION SHOULD STOP
```

---

# 127. Archive Decision

Archive when:

```text id="lbl074"
NO COMPELLING ROADMAP
+
OPPORTUNITY COST TOO HIGH
```

---

# 128. Archive Does Not Mean Delete

Preserve:

```text id="lbl075"
IP
Assets
Customer History
Collections
Metrics
Decision History
```

---

# 129. Zombie Label Rule

Canonical:

> **Every Label must be Active, Paused, Archived, or otherwise explicitly classified.**

No indefinite limbo.

---

# 130. Spin-Out

Spin-out means operational/organizational independence materially increases value.

Possible triggers:

```text id="lbl076"
SCALE
EXTERNAL CAPITAL
DEDICATED LEADERSHIP
PARTNERSHIP
RISK SEPARATION
CHANNEL NEED
```

---

# 131. Spin-Out Is Not Brand Graduation

A Label does not “win” by becoming separate.

Spin-out is a structural decision.

---

# 132. Spin-Out Requirements

Before spin-out:

```text id="lbl077"

Governance Plan
```

---

# 186. Governance Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

## Every Collection Gets a Brand

Creates fragmentation.

## Every Label Gets a Domain

Premature complexity.

## Every Label Gets Social Accounts

Attention fragmentation.

## Every Label Builds Its Own Backend

Destroys shared leverage.

## Founder Approves Every Asset Forever

Does not scale.

## Label P&L Is Unknown

Portfolio cannot allocate capital.

## Separate Legal Entities for Aesthetics

Unnecessary complexity.

## No Archive Policy

Zombie brands accumulate.

---

# 187. What Label Autonomy Must Not Mean

Autonomy does not mean:

```text id="lbl109"
NO STANDARDS
NO ECONOMIC DISCIPLINE
NO DATA SHARING
NO PORTFOLIO OVERSIGHT
```

It means:

> more decision rights within defined guardrails.
