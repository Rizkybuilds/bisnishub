# TeeStock Collection Framework v1.0

> **Canonical Originals Collection System**  
> Dokumen ini mendefinisikan anatomy, purpose, narrative, product architecture, SKU budget, sampling, launch model, merchandising, content, inventory, economics, experimentation, post-launch review, lifecycle, dan decision rules untuk setiap TeeStock Originals Collection.

---

# 1. Purpose

Collection Framework menjawab:

> **Bagaimana sebuah Originals Concept diterjemahkan menjadi release yang nyata, bisa dibeli, bisa diuji, dan bisa dipelajari tanpa langsung membangun sebuah brand penuh?**

Canonical principle:

> **A Collection is a controlled expression of an idea.**

---

# 2. Canonical Definition

> **Collection adalah cohesive group of products, creative assets, narrative, dan commercial activity yang digunakan untuk mengekspresikan serta menguji sebuah Originals Concept dalam periode atau context tertentu.**

Collection dapat menjadi:

```text
EXPERIMENT
RELEASE
CHAPTER
CAPSULE
SEASONAL EXPRESSION
```

tetapi tetap harus mempunyai purpose yang jelas.

---

# 3. Strategic Role

Collection mempunyai enam fungsi utama:

```text
EXPRESS THE CONCEPT
+
TEST MARKET DEMAND
+
CREATE PRODUCT
+
GENERATE DATA
+
BUILD AUDIENCE MEMORY
+
DEVELOP LABEL POTENTIAL
```

---

# 4. Collection Is Not Automatically a Brand

Critical distinction:

```text
CONCEPT
= the idea

COLLECTION
= a market expression of the idea

LABEL
= a repeatable consumer identity
```

Satu Collection dapat sukses tanpa perlu menjadi Label.

---

# 5. Collection Is Not Just a Product Group

Collection harus mempunyai cohesion.

Bukan:

```text
5 unrelated products
placed under one campaign name
```

Collection should connect through:

```text
IDEA
NARRATIVE
VISUAL LANGUAGE
PRODUCT LOGIC
```

---

# 6. Collection Architecture

Canonical:

```text
ORIGINALS CONCEPT
↓
COLLECTION
│
├── Narrative
├── Visual Direction
├── Product System
├── Artwork
├── Campaign
├── Merchandising
├── Inventory Plan
├── Experiment
└── Economics
```

---

# 7. Collection Types

Potential canonical types:

```text
CAPSULE
CHAPTER
SEASONAL
SPECIAL PROJECT
COLLABORATION
EVERGREEN
```

---

# 8. Capsule

Small collection designed primarily for validation.

Characteristics:

```text
FEW PRODUCTS
LOW CAPITAL
CLEAR HYPOTHESIS
SHORT LEARNING LOOP
```

Default model for new Originals Concepts.

---

# 9. Chapter

A narrative continuation within a Concept or Label.

Example:

```text
Chapter 001
Chapter 002
```

Useful when identity evolves through sequential stories.

---

# 10. Seasonal Collection

Built around a meaningful time/context.

Seasonal does not mean TeeStock must follow traditional fashion seasons.

---

# 11. Special Project

One-off exploration with:

- artist,
- cause,
- event,
- cultural moment.

It may remain intentionally non-repeatable.

---

# 12. Collaboration Collection

Created with external partner.

Must preserve:

```text
IP OWNERSHIP
REVENUE RULE
ATTRIBUTION
BRAND RELATIONSHIP
```

---

# 13. Evergreen Collection

A proven collection or product system that remains relevant beyond launch cycle.

Evergreen status should be earned.

---

# 14. Collection Brief

Every Collection must have a canonical brief before major execution.

Minimum:

```text
Collection ID
Concept
Working Title
Purpose
Belief
Audience
Narrative
Visual Direction
Product Strategy
Price Position
Launch Model
Inventory Model
Experiment Hypothesis
Budget
Success Signals
Kill / Iterate Signals
```

---

# 15. Collection Purpose

The brief must answer:

> **Why does this Collection need to exist?**

Weak:

> Karena butuh drop baru.

Strong:

> To test whether the "Work In Progress" identity can extend from a typography concept into a broader product world.

---

# 16. Narrative

Narrative explains:

```text
WHAT THIS COLLECTION IS ABOUT
WHY IT EXISTS NOW
WHAT IT MEANS TO THE AUDIENCE
```

It should not require a long manifesto to be understandable.

---

# 17. Narrative Hierarchy

Recommended:

```text
CORE IDEA
↓
COLLECTION MESSAGE
↓
PRODUCT EXPRESSIONS
↓
CONTENT
```

---

# 18. Narrative Coherence

Every major product should connect to the core idea.

Not every product must repeat identical wording.

---

# 19. Collection Title

Title can be:

- conceptual,
- sequential,
- descriptive.

It should support narrative without pretending to be a new brand.

---

# 20. Naming Rule

Avoid creating:

```text
Collection name
that visually behaves like
new independent brand
```

unless promotion to Label is intentional.

---

# 21. Product Architecture

A Collection may use:

```text
HERO PRODUCT
CORE SUPPORTING PRODUCT
EXPERIMENTAL PRODUCT
ACCESSORY
```

Not every role is mandatory.

---

# 22. Hero Product

Purpose:

```text
COMMUNICATE COLLECTION MOST CLEARLY
```

The Hero should be:

- visually strong,
- narratively strong,
- marketable,
- operationally viable.

---

# 23. Supporting Product

Extends the idea without competing for attention.

Examples:

- alternate graphic,
- secondary garment,
- accessory.

---

# 24. Experimental Product

Tests:

```text
NEW CATEGORY
NEW PRICE
NEW GARMENT
NEW DESIGN LANGUAGE
```

Keep risk isolated.

---

# 25. Product Count

Canonical principle:

> **Use the minimum number of products required to test the Collection thesis.**

More SKUs do not create stronger validation.

---

# 26. Initial Capsule Recommendation

Default conceptual model:

```text
1 HERO
+
1–2 SUPPORTING PRODUCTS
```

Not a hard rule.

---

# 27. SKU Budget

Every Collection should define:

```text
MAXIMUM ACCEPTABLE SKU COMPLEXITY
```

before launch.

---

# 28. SKU Budget Inputs

Consider:

```text
PRODUCTS
×
COLORS
×
SIZES
×
STOCK MODEL
```

---

# 29. Example

If:

```text
3 products
×
3 colors
×
6 sizes
```

then:

```text
54 possible variant combinations
```

This is already meaningful complexity.

---

# 30. Virtual vs Physical SKU

A Sellable Configuration does not need to equal pre-produced inventory.

Canonical strategy:

```text
VIRTUAL ASSORTMENT
can exceed
PHYSICAL FINISHED INVENTORY
```

where production model allows.

---

# 31. Shared Garment Platform

Early Collections should prefer approved TeeStock garment platforms.

This protects:

```text
CAPITAL
QUALITY
LEAD TIME
INVENTORY
```

---

# 32. Proprietary Product

Custom garment should enter Collection only when it materially supports:

```text
IDENTITY
PRODUCT DIFFERENTIATION
MARGIN
CUSTOMER VALUE
```

---

# 33. Product Selection Gate

Each product should answer:

```text
Does it express the concept?

Does the audience need it?

Can we make it reliably?

Does it justify its SKU complexity?

Does the economics work?
```

---

# 34. Artwork System

Artwork within a Collection should form a coherent system.

Could include:

```text
PRIMARY GRAPHIC
SECONDARY GRAPHIC
TYPE SYSTEM
SYMBOL
PATTERN
LABEL ELEMENT
```

---

# 35. Artwork Hierarchy

Not every artwork should be equally loud.

Collection may need:

```text
STATEMENT
+
SUBTLE
+
UTILITY
```

expressions.

---

# 36. Creative Repetition

Avoid simply placing the same phrase on:

- tee,
- hoodie,
- tote,
- cap

and calling it a Collection.

Product expressions should add value.

---

# 37. Visual Direction

Must define:

```text
COLOR
TYPE
IMAGE LANGUAGE
GRAPHIC SYSTEM
PHOTOGRAPHY
LAYOUT
```

at appropriate depth.

---

# 38. Collection Identity vs Label Identity

Collection can temporarily introduce:

- special color,
- special graphic,
- campaign treatment.

But if under a Label:

it must still feel like that Label.

---

# 39. Studio Role

TeeStock Studio may support:

```text
ARTWORK
PRODUCT DESIGN
MOCKUP
PACKAGING
CAMPAIGN ASSETS
```

Originals retains strategic ownership.

---

# 40. Sampling

Every physical Collection should use a defined sample strategy.

Possible:

```text
DIGITAL PROOF
PRODUCTION TEST
PHYSICAL SAMPLE
PRE-PRODUCTION SAMPLE
```

depending risk.

---

# 41. Sample Purpose

Sampling verifies:

```text
FIT
MATERIAL
COLOR
PLACEMENT
PRINT
PACKAGING
OVERALL FEEL
```

---

# 42. Sample Approval

No significant finished inventory should be produced before required sample approvals.

---

# 43. Sample Record

Should capture:

```text
Product
Version
Garment Platform
Artwork
Method
Reviewer
Result
Changes
Approval
```

---

# 44. Sample Iteration

If sample fails:

```text
ADJUST
↓
RESAMPLE IF NEEDED
↓
APPROVE
```

Do not normalize poor quality merely to hit launch date.

---

# 45. Production Readiness

Collection reaches production-ready when:

```text
PRODUCT SPEC COMPLETE
ARTWORK FINAL
BOM / RECIPE READY
PRICE KNOWN
SAMPLE APPROVED
SUPPLY READY
QC PROFILE READY
```

---

# 46. Launch Model

Collection can launch through:

```text
PREORDER
MADE TO ORDER
SMALL BATCH
READY STOCK
HYBRID
```

---

# 47. Preorder

Best when:

```text
DEMAND UNCERTAIN
+
AUDIENCE WILLING TO WAIT
```

Benefits:

- low inventory risk,
- demand validation.

---

# 48. Made to Order

Useful when shared garment/production infrastructure allows per-order production.

---

# 49. Small Batch

Useful for:

- faster delivery,
- physical scarcity,
- controlled risk.

---

# 50. Ready Stock

Best for proven demand.

Requires stronger confidence.

---

# 51. Hybrid Model

Example:

```text
Hero Product
→ small ready stock

Supporting Product
→ made to order
```

---

# 52. Inventory Plan

Collection brief should define:

```text
BASE GARMENT STOCK
FINISHED STOCK
PREORDER QTY
BUFFER
REORDER POLICY
```

---

# 53. Inventory Capital

Capital should match evidence level.

Early Collection should not consume disproportionate cash.

---

# 54. Reorder Rule

Do not reorder because:

> stock looks low.

Reorder based on:

```text
DEMAND VELOCITY
LEAD TIME
REMAINING CAMPAIGN WINDOW
CONTRIBUTION
COLLECTION LIFECYCLE
```

---

# 55. End-of-Collection Inventory

Before launch define:

> What happens if inventory remains?

Possible:

```text
KEEP AS EVERGREEN
MARKDOWN
BUNDLE
ARCHIVE
REWORK
```

---

# 56. Markdown

Markdown should be a deliberate inventory/economic decision.

It must not become default ending for every Collection.

---

# 57. Scarcity

If Collection says:

```text
LIMITED
```

there must be real limitation:

- quantity,
- time,
- production capacity.

---

# 58. Price Position

Each Collection should define intended price logic.

Price should align with:

```text
PRODUCT
AUDIENCE
LABEL / TEEStock POSITION
CONTRIBUTION
```

---

# 59. Price Consistency

Products within a Collection should have understandable price relationships.

---

# 60. Pricing Test

Collection can test price sensitivity.

But avoid changing too many variables simultaneously.

---

# 61. Collection Economics

Track:

```text
NET SALES
COGS
PRODUCTION
PACKAGING
FULFILLMENT
CREATIVE
CAMPAIGN
MARKETING
CONTRIBUTION
INVENTORY
```

---

# 62. Collection P&L

Every meaningful Collection should have its own management P&L.

Even before Label-level P&L exists.

---

# 63. Experiment Budget vs Operating Cost

Separate:

```text
LEARNING INVESTMENT
```

from:

```text
NORMAL PRODUCT ECONOMICS
```

where useful.

---

# 64. Launch Objective

Every launch must define its primary objective.

Possible:

```text
VALIDATE DEMAND
VALIDATE IDENTITY
GENERATE REVENUE
GROW LABEL
TEST CATEGORY
BUILD CUSTOMER BASE
```

---

# 65. One Primary Objective

A launch may have multiple benefits.

But choose one primary objective to avoid ambiguous evaluation.

---

# 66. Collection Experiment

Minimum:

```text
QUESTION
HYPOTHESIS
AUDIENCE
PRODUCT SET
CHANNEL
METRICS
GUARDRAILS
DECISION RULE
```

---

> Will the audience buy into the belief beyond a single graphic?

```text
same belief
```

# 69. Pre-Launch

```text
TEASER
STORY
WAITLIST
EARLY ACCESS
BEHIND THE SCENES
```

Only if useful.

---

# 70. Teaser Discipline

Do not create a month of hype for a tiny unproven capsule unless audience interest supports it.

---

# 71. Waitlist

Useful for:

```text
DEMAND SIGNAL
EARLY ACCESS
LAUNCH AUDIENCE
```

---

# 72. Early Access

Can reward:

- existing customers,
- subscribers,
- community.

Should not create unnecessary complexity.

---

# 73. Launch Day

Must ensure:

```text
PRODUCT LIVE
PRICE CORRECT
STOCK / PREORDER READY
CONTENT READY
PAYMENT READY
FULFILLMENT READY
```

---

# 74. Launch Readiness Gate

No launch if critical operational dependency is unresolved.

Marketing does not outrank fulfillment reality.

---

# 75. Content System

Collection content can include:

```text
IDEA
PRODUCT
DETAIL
PEOPLE
PROCESS
EDITORIAL
UGC
```

---

# 76. Idea Content

Explains belief/narrative.

Not necessarily product-focused.

---

# 77. Product Content

Explains:

- fit,
- material,
- artwork,
- details.

---

# 78. People Content

Shows product in context.

Can reinforce intended identity.

---

# 79. Process Content

Useful if it supports:

- craft,
- transparency,
- story.

Do not show process only because content calendar needs filler.

---

# 80. UGC

Post-launch UGC can provide:

- proof,
- community signal,
- fit context.

Rights/permission should be respected.

---

# 81. Content-to-Commerce Path

Canonical:

```text
CONTENT
↓
COLLECTION PAGE
↓
PRODUCT
↓
CHECKOUT
```

Minimize unnecessary friction.

---

# 82. Collection Landing Page

Should communicate:

```text
IDEA
VISUAL WORLD
PRODUCTS
ACTION
```

without hiding commerce behind excessive storytelling.

---

# 83. Merchandising

Collection may receive:

```text
Homepage Feature
Originals Feature
Collection Page
Product Cross-Sell
Email / CRM Placement
```

depending launch objective.

---

# 84. Collection vs Catalog

Collection groups products.

Catalog remains source of sellable product truth.

Do not create duplicate Product records for collection placement.

---

# 85. Product Priority

Within collection:

```text
HERO
SUPPORT
EXPERIMENT
```

can influence merchandising.

---

# 86. Channel Strategy

Early Originals should prioritize:

```text
TEEStock OWNED CHANNELS
```

for better:

- brand control,
- data,
- storytelling.

---

# 87. Marketplace

Can be used selectively.

Marketplace may validate product demand but often weakens story context.

---

# 88. Social Commerce

Can support discovery.

Canonical product/order data must still normalize into TeeStock systems.

---

# 89. Collection Customer Cohort

Buyers should form a traceable cohort.

Future question:

> How many Collection A customers bought Collection B?

---

# 90. First-Time Buyer

Track whether Collection attracts:

```text
NEW CUSTOMER
```

or mainly existing TeeStock audience.

---

# 91. Repeat Buyer

Track:

- TeeStock repeat buyer,
- Originals repeat buyer,
- Label repeat buyer.

These are different insights.

---

# 92. Cross-Sell

Potential:

```text
COLLECTION PRODUCT
→
RELATED COLLECTION
→
ESSENTIALS
```

if relevant.

---

# 93. Direct Demand

Track:

```text
Collection Name Search
Label Search
Direct Page Visits
Email Click
Organic Social Click
```

where possible.

---



---

# 95. Sell-Through

Useful for stocked collections.

Should be interpreted relative to:

- inventory quantity,
- time window,
- replenishment.

---

# 96. Conversion

Low conversion may indicate:

- concept,
- product,
- price,
- trust,
- page,
- traffic mismatch.

Do not diagnose from conversion alone.

---

# 97. High Demand + Weak Contribution

Not automatically a scale decision.

Collection economics must improve or price/cost must change.

---

# 98. High Engagement + Low Purchase

Potential:

```text
STRONG CONTENT
WEAK PRODUCT OFFER
PRICE ISSUE
AUDIENCE MISMATCH
```

Needs diagnosis.

---

# 99. Low Traffic + High Conversion

Potential strong product with insufficient distribution.

---

# 100. High Sales + Weak Repeat

May indicate product hit rather than brand potential.

---

# 101. High Returns

Can invalidate apparent success.

Analyze:

```text
FIT
QUALITY
EXPECTATION
FULFILLMENT
```

---

# 102. Post-Launch Review

Every Collection should close a formal review.

Canonical:

```text
WHAT WE EXPECTED
WHAT HAPPENED
WHAT WORKED
WHAT FAILED
WHY
WHAT WE LEARNED
WHAT WE DO NEXT
```

---

# 103. Review Timing

Review can happen:

```text
EARLY SIGNAL REVIEW
+
FINAL COLLECTION REVIEW
```

if launch period is long enough.

---

# 104. Early Signal Review

Used to detect:

- broken page,
- pricing issue,
- stock issue,
- operational failure.

Not necessarily to make brand promotion decision immediately.

---

# 105. Final Review

Evaluates collection against original hypothesis.

---

# 106. Decision Outputs

Possible:

```text
REPEAT
ITERATE
EXPAND
EVERGREEN
PROMOTE TO LABEL CANDIDATE
CLOSE
ARCHIVE
```

---

# 107. Repeat

Use when:

> hypothesis deserves another similar test.

---

# 108. Iterate

Use when:

> concept promising, execution weak.

---

# 109. Expand

Use when:

> demand is proven and more product/channel depth is justified.

---

# 110. Evergreen

Use when product remains relevant and demand is stable.

---

# 111. Promote to Label Candidate

Only if evidence supports world-building beyond Collection.

---

# 112. Close

Stop active selling.

Does not necessarily mean delete assets/history.

---

# 113. Archive

Preserve:

- data,
- creative,
- learnings,
- IP records.

---

---

# 120. Live

---

# 121. Evaluating

Primary sales window ended; review in progress.

---

# 122. Evergreen

Collection/product remains active beyond launch campaign.

---

# 123. Closed

No new sales intended.

---

# 124. Archived

Operationally inactive but preserved.

---

# 125. Collection Versioning

Material changes in:

- product system,
- narrative,
- creative direction

may create a new Collection/Chapter rather than silently changing the old one.

---

# 126. Second Collection

For Label Candidate, second Collection is a major test.

Question:

> **Can the identity create another compelling expression?**

---

# 127. Second-Collection Discipline

Do not simply:

```text
change color
+
reuse graphic
```

and treat it as proof of brand depth.

---

# 128. Collection Continuity

New Collection should balance:

```text
RECOGNITION
+
NEWNESS
```

---

# 129. Recognition

Audience should still understand the underlying world.

---

# 130. Newness

There must be enough evolution to justify another release.

---

# 131. Collection Cannibalization

Multiple live Collections may compete for:

- audience,
- inventory,
- marketing attention.

Manage calendar intentionally.

---

# 132. Release Calendar

Originals should eventually coordinate:

```text
DEVELOPMENT
SAMPLE
CONTENT
LAUNCH
FULFILLMENT
REVIEW
```

across Collections.

---

# 133. Avoid Launch Overlap

Too many launches simultaneously reduce learning and execution quality.

---

# 134. Collection WIP Limit

Maintain limited concurrent:

```text
DEVELOPMENT
LIVE
EVALUATION
```

Collections.

---

# 135. Collaboration Collection

```text

IP

TERM
```

---

# 136. Collaboration Economics

Track separately:

```text
Partner Share
Royalty
Creative Cost
Marketing Contribution
```

---

# 137. Collaboration Signal

A successful collaboration does not prove standalone Label demand.

Separate:

```text
PARTNER-DRIVEN DEMAND
```

from:

```text
LABEL-DRIVEN DEMAND
```

---

# 138. Special Event Collection

Event-based sales may be temporary by design.

Do not force evergreen strategy onto inherently temporary release.

---

# 139. Collection Packaging

Special packaging should exist only if it:

```text
IMPROVES STORY
CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE
PRODUCT PROTECTION
```

enough to justify cost/complexity.

---

# 140. Base Packaging Default

Use shared TeeStock packaging unless collection-specific experience materially matters.

---

# 141. Collection Insert

Low-cost insert/card may provide narrative without custom packaging complexity.

---

# 142. Samples as Content

Sampling process may generate content.

But failed/internal samples should not misrepresent final product.

---

# 143. Product Photography

Collection photography should show:

```text
PRODUCT TRUTH
+
COLLECTION WORLD
```

Both matter.

---

# 144. Editorial Image

Can build identity.

But must not replace clear product images.

---

# 145. Collection Page Structure

```text

HERO VISUAL
↓
PRODUCTS
↓
STORY
↓
```

# 146. Collection Copy

Should follow Originals voice:

```text
EDITORIAL
IDEA-DRIVEN
CULTURAL
```

but remain clear enough to buy product.

---

# 147. Brand Claim Discipline

Do not make philosophical claims that product/brand cannot support.

Depth should be earned.

---

# 148. Campaign Message

A Collection should have one clear primary message.

Not five competing themes.

# 149. Campaign Asset System

Possible:

```text
HERO
PRODUCT
```

# 150. Paid Media

Use if useful for:

- demand validation,
- scale.

Track paid demand separately from organic demand.

# 151. Paid Test Guardrail

Do not conclude:

> brand has organic demand

when conversion came almost entirely from paid reach.

# 152. Marketing Spend Cap

Capsule should have controlled campaign budget.

Avoid spending past the learning value of the experiment.

---

# 153. Inventory + Marketing Coordination

No reason to accelerate demand if fulfillment/inventory cannot support it.

# 171. Collection Failure Modes

## Too Many SKUs

Signal becomes noisy and capital risk rises.

## Story Without Product

Beautiful campaign, weak offer.

## Product Without Idea

Becomes generic merch.

## Huge Launch Before Validation

Capital wasted.

## No Hypothesis

Cannot learn.

## No Post-Launch Review

Same mistakes repeat.

## One Product Hit = Label

Premature promotion.

## Permanent Discounts

Distorted validation.

---

# 172. What a Collection Must Not Become

## Temporary Brand Proliferation

Collection is not automatically its own brand.

## Creative Vanity Project

Market evidence matters.

## Inventory Dump

Product quantity must be intentional.

## Campaign-Only Artifact

It must produce learning.

## Unmeasured Experiment

Decision requires evidence.

# 173. Collection Success Definition

A Collection succeeds when it creates enough evidence to make the next decision confidently.

That decision may be:

```text
SCALE
ITERATE
REPEAT
EVERGREEN
PROMOTE
STOP
```

Revenue is important, but learning quality is also part of success.
